Does rewarding honesty fix overconfident models?

A model trained to be right tends to become more sure of itself than it should be. RLCD is the idea of rewarding the probabilities it states instead. This is a pre-registered, open test of whether that works, including the results that go against it.

Try it: be right, or be honest

A toy where the true probabilities are known. The reward is a mix of two things: the plain "was the answer right?" reward (RLVR) and the RLCD reward, which pays for stating the true probability. Drag the first slider and watch what training does to the model's confidence.

The idea in one picture

The model reads a state and a typed question and states a probability for every option in one pass. RLVR rewards the sampled answer for being correct, which is maximised by putting all the probability on the likeliest answer, so the model becomes sharper than the evidence justifies. RLCD scores the whole distribution with a proper scoring rule, which pays most for stating the true probabilities. When only the sampled answer can be checked, dividing the reward by that answer's probability keeps the gradient unbiased.

The sceptic's objection. With fixed labels, rewarding the full distribution has the same gradient as ordinary supervised training. So maybe RLCD is just supervised fine-tuning plus a temperature fitted afterwards. The plan was frozen before any full run, and every experiment compares RLCD with exactly that sceptical baseline.

2 · A 9B language model

The sceptic mostly wins

A 9B model trained on teacher-labelled questions, three seeds per arm, tested on an independent set of unseen domains. RLVR is more overconfident than RLCD, but plain supervised training does as well as RLCD, and after one fitted temperature the arms are close.

3 · Breakout

A narrow, fragile win for RLCD

Here outcomes depend on the model's own moves, the case where RL is actually needed. Training beliefs together with play beat the sceptic's version (fit the beliefs by supervised learning, then scale) by the pre-registered rule. But the sceptic's version was better in {{b4_better_seeds}} of 3 seeds, and without its temperature step it is nearly as good.

The honest bottom line

RLCD fixes RLVR's overconfidence, but gave no clear advantage over supervised training plus temperature scaling, except possibly in the on-policy Breakout case, where the evidence is weak.

What this does not show. One model, one teacher-written dataset, one game, one learning rate shared across the arms, and a KL penalty that already limits RLVR. The test set was written by the same teacher as the training data. It says nothing about RLCD in general. The results that went against a hypothesis are listed by name in the repository.
