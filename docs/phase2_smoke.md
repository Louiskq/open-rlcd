## What it shows

* **Nothing diverged.** All eight runs finished with finite losses; KL to the start model grew smoothly.
* **How noisy a single run is.** An exact rerun of A1 (same seed, same data order) differs from the first run by
  0.015 in selection log loss, 0.012 in dev log loss and 0.008 in selection ECE: bf16 kernels are not bit-reproducible
  and trajectories diverge a little. **That sets the scale for reading every other row.**
* **H2 roughly holds.** A5 (the analytic-gradient path) against A1 (autograd): the same accuracy on both splits
  (0.7578, 0.8671), selection log loss 0.524 against 0.538 and dev 0.308 against 0.319, which is no larger than the
  rerun's own difference from A1. The two gradients agree to about 1e-8 in the unit tests and the per-type
  gradient shares match; the remaining gap is run-to-run noise. The formal test needs δ (D13).
* **A4 (RLCD-bandit, unclipped) is indistinguishable from A1** on every metric, within that noise.
* **A3 (RLVR) shows the H1 pattern on the selection split.** Accuracy within about half a point of A1 (0.753 against
  0.758 selection, 0.874 against 0.867 dev), but about 2–3× the selection ECE (0.075 against 0.026–0.034), a log loss
  0.07–0.09 higher, and fitted temperatures of 2.16 (NOUL) and 1.36 (CHOICE), the over-confidence signature; after
  temperature scaling its selection ECE (0.027) matches A1's. **On dev the raw differences are inside the noise at this
  length** (ECE 0.026 against 0.015–0.023), so H1 is not settled; the full-length runs are the test.
* **A6 against A6r: no conclusion.** A6 is better on selection log loss (0.488 against 0.521, about twice the rerun
  noise) but not on dev (0.319 against 0.314). One seed, a tenth of the steps, and a candidate pool of only 1,028
  questions (150 selected per round) so mining had little to choose from. H4 predicts a null; this is not evidence
  either way.
* **The start model (A0) is miscalibrated in the way the design assumed**: fitted temperatures 2.34 (NOUL), 1.43
  (CHOICE), 1.02 (SCORE), and serving it at its stored 1.05 changes almost nothing. Continuing it with a proper loss
  alone brings the fitted temperatures to about 1 (0.7–1.1), so after 73 steps A1 is already close to calibrated.

## Gradient shares by answer type (D4)

The share of an optimiser step's gradient norm taken by each type was measured at four steps per run (A3, A4 below).
**It is noisy**: micro-batches are formed by length bucket, so some steps contain a single type (at step 50 both runs
are 100% SCORE), and four steps cannot give a stable figure. The averages, which should be read as indicative only:
SCORE carries **0.47 (A3) and 0.43 (A4)** of the gradient norm against **0.17** of the questions in those steps, NOUL
0.17 / 0.23 against 0.24, CHOICE 0.36 / 0.34 against 0.59. That is the *opposite* of what D4 anticipated (SCORE getting
less gradient than its share) for the sampled-answer arms. The supervised arms were measured with a flawed single-
micro-batch version first (SCORE 1% to 64% across steps) and are not reported; the three full-length A1 sweep runs
measure at 4 steps each, which gives 12 points for A1.

## Things that went wrong, and what was done

* A first version of the gradient-share measurement used one micro-batch and fluctuated wildly; replaced by a
  whole-step measurement before any number was reported.
* A review of the runner found that A6r would have crashed on its first selection (a Python `random.Random` was passed
  where a numpy `Generator` is needed). Fixed, and the selection round was pulled out of the training closure into
  `select_round` with tests (labels hidden from the scorer, A6r never scoring, identical per-type quotas for A6 and
  A6r, no item chosen twice).
* An SSH drop killed the three runs of the second wave at step 20; they were relaunched fully detached
  (`scripts/expA_wave.sh`, `setsid`) and completed.
* The stored temperature of this checkpoint is **1.05**, not the 1.30 written in earlier docs (that was the 2B model's);
  `docs/decisions.md` D2 carries the correction.
* The first launch of the Breakout validation ran out of GPU memory next to two training runs; relaunched when
  memory was free.

## A1 learning-rate sweep (full length, seed 0)

Three full-length A1 runs (734 steps) finished; none diverged. The rule (`scripts/pick_lr.py`: lowest log loss on the
**selection** split, diverged runs excluded) was fixed before any of them ran. Selection-split log loss, per answer
type:

| learning rate | all | NOUL | CHOICE | SCORE | selection accuracy (all types: NOUL / CHOICE / SCORE) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5e-5 | 0.367 | 0.327 | 0.349 | 0.566 | 0.860 / 0.857 / 0.824 |
| **1e-4 (chosen)** | **0.341** | 0.308 | 0.312 | 0.569 | 0.855 / 0.872 / 0.810 |
| 2e-4 | 0.360 | 0.305 | 0.349 | 0.575 | 0.862 / 0.855 / 0.775 |

The start model's selection log loss is 0.699, so full-length training takes it to about half. **The differences
between rates (0.02 to 0.026) are about the size of the same-seed rerun noise measured at 10% length (0.015)**, so 1e-4
is the pre-declared choice, not a sharp optimum; 2e-4 is best on NOUL, 1e-4 on CHOICE, and SCORE is flat in log loss
while its accuracy drops with the rate (0.824, 0.810, 0.775).

**Gradient share by answer type, supervised A1** (12 measurements: four steps in each of the three runs; steps 2, 100,
300 and 600): NOUL **0.46** of the gradient norm for 0.46 of the questions, CHOICE **0.44** for 0.40, SCORE **0.10** for
0.15 (range 0.01 to 0.25). So for the supervised loss SCORE gets *less* gradient than its share of questions, the
direction D4 anticipated, whereas the 4-point smoke measurements for the sampled-answer arms A3/A4 showed SCORE above its
share (0.43–0.47 for 0.17). Both are noisy and at different training stages; they are descriptive only.
